## Mon Nov 16 · Text insight · no image

Most practice websites are built for the owner. Not for the person visiting for the first time.

The owner wants to see the mission statement, the team photo, the story of how the practice started.

The first-time visitor wants three answers in about five seconds:

1. Do you help with my problem?
2. Are you near me, and do you take my insurance?
3. How do I book?

When those answers are buried under an "About us" paragraph, the visitor goes back to Google and picks the next result.

A quick test: open your homepage on your phone. Without scrolling, can a stranger tell what you treat, where you are, and how to book?

If not, that is the first thing to fix. It costs far less than more ads, and it makes every ad you run work better.

## Tue Nov 17 · Teardown · image: 2026-11-17-tue.png

What we usually find when we audit an acupuncture clinic's website.

This is a composite of the patterns we see most often, not one specific clinic:

1. One "Services" page listing 12 treatments in a single paragraph. Google and AI tools can't tell what the clinic is known for.
2. No page answering the questions patients actually search, like "does acupuncture help with migraines" or "is acupuncture covered by insurance."
3. The address and phone number differ between the website, Google Business Profile and Yelp.
4. Booking needs three clicks and a separate login.
5. 40+ great reviews on Google, and none of them on the website.

None of these needs a full rebuild. Most clinics fix all five in a few weeks, and it shows up in both Google rankings and AI answers.

## Wed Nov 18 · Data point · image: 2026-11-18-wed.png

53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than 3 seconds to load.

That figure comes from Google's research on mobile sites, and in our experience it holds for small practices too.

Think about who is searching on a phone. Someone in pain, between meetings, deciding fast. They tap your listing, wait, and tap back.

You never see them in your bookings. You only see a high bounce rate in your analytics, if you check at all.

The usual causes are easy to fix:
- Huge uncompressed photos on the homepage
- Too many plugins or tracking scripts
- Cheap hosting that slows down at busy hours

Run your homepage through Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool this week. If the mobile score is under 50, speed is costing you clients.

## Thu Nov 19 · Checklist · image: 2026-11-19-thu.png

7 things every service page needs before Google or ChatGPT will trust it.

1. One service per page. "Anxiety therapy" and "couples counseling" each get their own.
2. A clear headline that says what it is and where: "Anxiety therapy in Austin, TX."
3. Who it's for, in plain words.
4. What to expect at the first visit.
5. Answers to 3-5 real questions patients ask.
6. Proof: a review, a credential, a result.
7. One obvious next step: book, call or message.

Search engines and AI assistants both look for pages that clearly answer a specific need for a specific place. A page with all seven gives them exactly that.

Save this for your next website update.
